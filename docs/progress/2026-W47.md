## 4. Important Design and Technical Decisions

- I kept partial terrain damage between strikes so the player could move away and resume digging. Terrain HP and passability are separate, and clearing a tile opens a route for the player and enemies. Holding the mouse button became a way to keep digging as the player advanced.
- I wanted reproducible seeds and varied regions. I asked for a transition band leading into a larger area of harder terrain. Biome effects are separate from terrain hardness. Plains accelerated producers, highlands helped tower range and sight, lakes shaped detours, and fog preserved the record of explored terrain.
- I limited placement to the eight cells surrounding the player so extending a defense requires moving with it. A rejected placement explains the restriction and preserves the card. Completed research survives the loss of the technology facility during a run.
- The early expedition had 8 currency, four wall cards, two tower cards, one producer card, and the starting facility. Card draws cost 3 and research cost 10. I kept Baseline, A, and B for comparison and adopted Challenge to see what the player could earn before the first attack. In `6f4fc51`, Challenge started with a basic tool and facility but no currency or ordinary cards. It paid 0.1 currency per actual point of digging damage and guaranteed a wall or tower among the first three paid crafts.
- For the P0-H-FIX02 follow-up, I removed the initial Challenge facility so construction is part of earning the first defense. I set trial costs of 10 for a facility, 20 for level-two tool research, and 3 for a card. Challenge keeps the 60-second preparation window and the defense-card guarantee. Completed research survives facility loss and paid relocation.
- For FIX03, I chose W25 as the first playtest candidate and kept the original 50 HP and structure attributes as the Control. I want to compare the value of compact defenses with the coverage and redundancy of separate buildings. All experimental values are provisional.
- For FIX04, I asked to test how a mature defense responds to later waves and whether income outpaces useful spending. I kept the original Challenge rules as the default and asked for separate combat and economy experiments so I can compare their effects.
- I chose Challenge, W25, and Combined so course playtesters would all start from one set of rules. Combined couples denser releases and armored units with tiered card prices and a reduced reward after the eighth kill from a wave. The earlier profiles stayed available for comparisons. The selected values were provisional.
- With automatic merging, the fourth placement could remove three firing or blocking cells before the player had decided how to use them. I made merging a separate action to let players choose when to take that trade-off. The preview uses a visible approach or a known entrance. It withholds a numeric comparison when explored terrain or the target is insufficient.
- A mining-focused route could win under the old arrival condition. I wanted the northward route to set up a final defense, so I required the device to be activated and both final cohorts to resolve. Separate contact checks keep a nearby player or device from taking damage across an obstacle.

## 6. Problems Encountered

I found repeated mining inputs cumbersome and some card rewards easy to miss. Some maps had too much open space or concentrated rocks, and the strip of harder terrain felt like an artificial gate. I also found remote building placement too permissive and the early waves too weak. I prioritized clearer interaction feedback, more varied terrain, closer placement, and stronger opening-wave options.

Greater durability increased structural pressure in a matched browser defense test. More frequent tower kills also increased income in some A scenarios. I still want to check in human playtests whether that extra pressure creates harder decisions for the player. The payoff from upgrading buildings, long-session pacing, sustained performance, and replayability remain open. The production build reports a Vite chunk-size warning. Recorded browser verification covers Windows desktop Edge.
