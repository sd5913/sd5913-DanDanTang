## 4. Important Design and Technical Decisions

I wanted excavation to create a trade-off. A route that helps the player advance can also give enemies a way in.

- I wanted walls to spend numerical durability to stop enemies. Each valid hit would consume the attacking enemy and reduce the wall's HP. Opening a gap could let the next attacker through. The wall impact would earn no tower-kill income.
- The design proposed merging four orthogonally connected buildings of the same type and level. The earliest placed cell would keep the upgrade, and the other three would become available. The merge trigger and higher-level recipes were still open.
- I wanted to connect tower kills and producers to spending on building cards and research. The design covered walls, towers, and income buildings in the card supply. The technology facility and run-wide research had their own rules. The exact card-draw model and facility rebuild costs were still open.
- Spring Plains and Winter Highlands were ideas for regions with different income and defense trade-offs. I also considered moving forward defenses and operating more than one base.

## 6. Problems Encountered

I wanted to preserve building freedom while keeping progression possible. The design identified risks around blocked routes, enemies unable to navigate, random access to key buildings, merges that open defensive gaps, and passive play encouraged by unrestricted construction.

## 7. Changes to the Development Plan

I shifted the concept from building defenses on a fixed map to letting players shape the map through excavation. The GDD outlines a prototype beginning with movement, collision, grid destruction, and a reachable destination.
