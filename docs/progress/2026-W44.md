## 1. Weekly Goals

I wanted the survival tower defense concept to have a distinct mechanic, with clear rules and a scope small enough to prototype.

## 2. Completed Tasks and Development Progress

GDD v0.2 is dated 31 October 2026. It records a square-grid world that players can excavate from a southern start toward a northern exit. Digging creates space for movement and construction, and enemies can use the routes players open. The design includes indestructible obstacles, harder terrain, tool progression, and pressure from a fixed southern enemy entrance.

In my recollection, *DDTank* inspired the idea of destructible terrain. I considered letting players move or abandon defenses as they dug deeper. The dated GDD establishes the scope of the design as of 31 October.

## 4. Important Design and Technical Decisions

I wanted excavation to create a trade-off. A route that helps the player advance can also give enemies a way in.

- GDD v0.2 proposes merging four orthogonally connected buildings of the same type and level. The earliest placed cell keeps the upgraded building, and the other three become available.
- The design uses currency-funded, randomized building cards for walls, towers, and economic buildings. It handles the technology facility and research separately, with completed research lasting for the current run.
- Spring Plains and Winter Highlands were explored as future regions with different income and defensive characteristics. Secondary bases were part of that exploration.

## 6. Problems Encountered

I wanted to preserve building freedom while keeping progression possible. The design identified risks around blocked routes, enemies unable to navigate, random access to key buildings, merges that open defensive gaps, and passive play encouraged by unrestricted construction.

## 7. Changes to the Development Plan

I shifted the concept from building defenses on a fixed map to letting players shape the map through excavation. The GDD outlines a prototype beginning with movement, collision, grid destruction, and a reachable destination.
